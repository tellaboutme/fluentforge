# CEFR Curriculum Model

## Principle

The official CEFR uses can-do descriptors across language activities and competences. FluentForge maps those outcomes into teachable prerequisites and observable evidence. It does not pretend that completing a list automatically awards a level.

## Level transition logic

A level estimate requires:

- sufficient breadth across required outcomes;
- repeated evidence over time;
- at least two task formats for major skills;
- some unseen transfer tasks;
- no critical prerequisite gaps;
- confidence above the configured threshold.

## Planning vocabulary ranges

These are product-planning bands, not official CEFR thresholds. Count word families/lemmas and multiword expressions separately, and store receptive/productive mastery separately.

| Level | Suggested cumulative receptive band | Suggested cumulative productive band |
|---|---:|---:|
| A1 | 600–1,000 | 350–600 |
| A2 | 1,500–2,500 | 900–1,500 |
| B1 | 2,750–4,000 | 1,800–2,750 |
| B2 | 4,000–6,000 | 2,750–4,000 |
| C1 | 7,000–10,000 | 4,500–7,000 |
| C2 | 10,000–16,000+ | 7,000–10,000+ |

The system should prefer high-frequency words, meanings, collocations, and chunks relevant to the learner's goals. Depth and usable phrase knowledge matter as much as raw count.

## Approximate effort bands

Do not promise a fixed completion time. Store these only as planning ranges and update the forecast from observed learning rate:

- A1 → A2: foundation and survival independence
- A2 → B1: major transition from rehearsed language to independent everyday use
- B1 → B2: broader fluency, argument, workplace and media competence
- B2 → C1: precision, complexity, implicit meaning, academic/professional flexibility
- C1 → C2: exceptional breadth, nuance, speed, style, and synthesis

The C-level stages often take much longer than early levels and require extensive authentic use.

## Level summaries

### A1 — Basic survival

- understand and use very common concrete expressions;
- exchange basic personal information;
- read short notices and forms;
- write simple phrases and linked sentences;
- produce intelligible rehearsed speech.

### A2 — Routine independence

- handle predictable everyday transactions;
- describe background, routines, needs, and recent events;
- understand short clear messages and texts;
- maintain short conversations with support;
- write simple connected messages.

### B1 — Independent everyday user

- understand main points of clear standard input on familiar matters;
- handle most common travel and daily situations;
- produce connected speech and writing;
- explain experiences, plans, opinions, and reasons;
- recover from communication problems.

### B2 — Confident independent user

- understand complex input in concrete and abstract domains;
- interact with reasonable fluency and spontaneity;
- develop and defend viewpoints;
- write clear detailed texts across genres;
- use broader vocabulary and grammar with control.

### C1 — Flexible proficient user

- understand demanding extended input and implicit meaning;
- speak fluently with little obvious searching;
- adapt language for social, academic, and professional purposes;
- produce well-structured detailed communication on complex subjects;
- use register, cohesion, and nuance effectively.

### C2 — Highly proficient user

- understand almost all forms of spoken and written language with ease;
- synthesise and reconstruct information from multiple sources;
- express meaning spontaneously, precisely, and flexibly;
- distinguish fine shades of meaning in complex situations;
- control style, rhetoric, idiom, and reformulation at a very high level.
