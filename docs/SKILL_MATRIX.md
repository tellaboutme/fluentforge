# Complete Skill Matrix

The platform must model language as a network of competencies, not one score.

## 1. Listening

- detect words in connected speech;
- understand gist;
- identify specific details;
- follow instructions and sequences;
- infer speaker attitude, intention, and relationship;
- distinguish fact, opinion, irony, uncertainty, and emphasis;
- follow conversations with turn-taking and interruptions;
- understand narratives, interviews, meetings, lectures, debates, and media;
- tolerate unknown words without losing the message;
- understand accent and speed variation;
- take useful notes;
- verify comprehension and request clarification.

## 2. Spoken production

- introduce and describe;
- narrate past events;
- explain processes;
- compare and evaluate;
- state and support opinions;
- speculate and qualify claims;
- present structured arguments;
- summarise sources;
- deliver prepared and spontaneous presentations;
- reformulate precisely when a word is missing;
- maintain extended discourse.

## 3. Spoken interaction

- open, maintain, and close conversations;
- ask and answer follow-up questions;
- take, hold, yield, and recover a turn;
- negotiate plans and meaning;
- clarify and repair misunderstandings;
- agree, disagree, interrupt, and soften appropriately;
- participate in interviews, meetings, debates, and collaborative tasks;
- adapt to formality, relationship, and context.

## 4. Pronunciation and phonology

- consonant and vowel contrasts that affect intelligibility;
- syllable structure and endings;
- word stress;
- sentence stress and focus;
- rhythm and weak forms;
- linking, assimilation, elision, and reductions;
- intonation for statements, questions, attitude, politeness, and contrast;
- chunking speech into meaningful units;
- comprehensibility across accents;
- self-monitoring and targeted repair.

The goal is intelligibility and flexible control—not erasing the learner's identity or forcing a native accent.

## 5. Reading

- decode accurately and efficiently;
- skim for gist;
- scan for facts;
- understand reference, connectors, and paragraph structure;
- infer word meaning from context and morphology;
- identify purpose, audience, stance, bias, tone, and implication;
- follow arguments and evidence;
- compare sources;
- read instructions, messages, articles, documentation, academic prose, and literature;
- annotate, summarise, and critically evaluate.

## 6. Written production

- accurate sentences;
- paragraph unity and development;
- descriptions, narratives, explanations, reviews, reports, essays, proposals, and creative texts;
- thesis, argument, evidence, counterargument, and conclusion;
- coherence and cohesive devices;
- appropriate register and tone;
- precise vocabulary and controlled complexity;
- planning, drafting, revising, proofreading, and editing.

## 7. Written interaction

- chats, comments, forms, emails, requests, support messages, applications, and professional correspondence;
- respond to another person's content rather than writing in isolation;
- manage politeness, directness, urgency, and relationship;
- ask for clarification and confirm shared understanding.

## 8. Vocabulary and phraseology

- form, meaning, pronunciation, spelling, grammar, register, and frequency;
- receptive recognition vs productive recall;
- multiple senses and polysemy;
- collocations;
- lexical chunks and sentence frames;
- phrasal verbs;
- idioms and colloquialisms;
- word families and derivation;
- synonyms, antonyms, connotation, and precision;
- topic, academic, professional, and technical vocabulary;
- metaphor and figurative language;
- dictionary and corpus skills.

Vocabulary targets in this repository are planning estimates, not official CEFR definitions. The platform should estimate breadth, depth, automaticity, and productive availability separately.

## 9. Grammar

- morphology and inflection;
- tense, aspect, and time reference;
- modality;
- questions and negation;
- noun phrases, determiners, countability, and agreement;
- pronouns and reference;
- adjectives, adverbs, and comparison;
- prepositions and particles;
- clause structure and word order;
- coordination and subordination;
- relative, conditional, participle, and infinitive clauses;
- passive and causative structures;
- reported speech;
- information structure and emphasis;
- advanced patterns, ellipsis, inversion, and stylistic choice.

Track grammar as form–meaning–use, not only rule recall.

## 10. Fluency and automaticity

- retrieval speed;
- pause frequency and placement;
- chunk length;
- speech rate appropriate to task;
- ability to continue despite gaps;
- automatic use of common structures;
- rapid comprehension of high-frequency language;
- balance between speed and accuracy.

## 11. Discourse and cohesion

- sequencing and signposting;
- reference chains;
- topic development;
- given/new information;
- paragraph and turn organization;
- connectors and rhetorical relations;
- genre conventions;
- concise vs elaborated expression;
- reformulation, examples, and emphasis.

## 12. Pragmatics and sociolinguistics

- speech acts: requesting, apologising, refusing, suggesting, persuading, complaining;
- directness and mitigation;
- politeness and face management;
- formal, neutral, informal, and colloquial register;
- humour, irony, understatement, and implicature;
- cultural variation without stereotyping;
- inclusive and respectful communication.

## 13. Mediation

- summarise spoken or written information;
- explain a difficult idea more simply;
- relay key information between people;
- translate meaning informally without word-for-word dependence;
- combine information from sources;
- facilitate collaboration and resolve communication gaps;
- adapt content to audience knowledge and needs.

## 14. Learning strategies

- goal setting;
- noticing and recording language;
- retrieval practice;
- spaced review;
- interleaving;
- using context before translation;
- seeking and using feedback;
- maintaining an error log;
- self-assessing confidence;
- selecting comprehensible but challenging input;
- reflecting and adjusting plans.
